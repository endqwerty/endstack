# Ketchup

Catch the user up on what changed in this chat since the last message they wrote before the catch-up request. Notifications and subagent results are not their messages. Use the bro style: plain, direct, conversational language, without restating the request. Assume the user remembers their last message and nothing after it. Call each PR, branch, or agent by a name they would know, not by a label you invented during the work.

## Gather what changed

Keep this turn read-only. Make visuals only from what you already have. Do not change code, start an app or a job, or post anything to make them. Collect your own work since that message, reports from subagents, and messages from anyone else in this chat. Check the current state of each PR, CI run, or agent this chat touched. Say which are still running and which you could not check. Check each number you show against its source, and say when you correct one.

An action item is a decision or step only the user can take, such as a product choice, an approval, an irreversible action, or access you lack. Include any still waiting on the user, even one raised before their last message. Work you can still do yourself is not an action item. Never invent a call to fill the reply. An unrequested step the work does not need is not an action item, unless it is irreversible and waiting on the user. Give that exception one short sentence and no visual, instead of the decision brief below.

One call can cover many items, such as a set of PRs waiting for merge approval. Collect each item's link, what changes for users, the evidence behind your recommendation, its risk, and your recommendation. Say "unknown" for a needed fact you do not know. Leave out a link you do not have.

## Write the reply

Open with one sentence that says how many action items there are, or that there are none. If nothing happened and nothing waits on the user, say so and stop.

Then show existing captures inline when they help explain what changed. Say what happened, most important first. Give each result and what it means for the user. Skip steps that changed nothing. Name in one line any work left that you can do yourself.

Then list the action items as plain bullets. Never cut items or shrink them to meet a word limit. A count cannot replace a list, and a link to a report cannot replace an item. Spend words on the calls before the story. For the unrequested irreversible-step exception above, use only one short sentence and no visual. Give other action items a short executive summary. Cover the decision to make, why it matters now, the options and what each costs, your recommendation, and what happens if the user does not answer. If you do not know a part, say so instead of guessing. Link to the detail when there is one, and write the summary so the user can decide without opening it.

When one call covers several items, state the shared call and recommendation once, then give every item its own bullet, riskiest first. Each bullet has its link and name, user impact, evidence, and risk, even when low. State its recommendation if it differs from the shared one. The reply alone must let the user decide each item. Include prerequisites and irreversible consequences in your recommendation. For a choice, give every option considered, what it gains and costs, and the current state when known.

## Visuals

Reuse screenshots and videos the work already produced. When a visible change has no capture, say "No screenshot yet" and explain what changed on screen. Never draw or generate a picture of a screen you did not capture. Link only artifacts that exist.

When a picture explains better than sentences, draw a small factual diagram or chart from the evidence you have. Use it for relationships, a dependency chain, a before and after, or options side by side. Never invent numbers or draw a status list, action-item list, or unconnected boxes. Keep labels plain and visuals readable at a glance. Do not repeat a fact in text and a visual, or across visuals. When captures already show options, put each option's gain and cost under its capture instead of drawing another diagram.

## Slides

Use the screenshots, recordings, diffs, and data the work produced, not the chat's prose about them. Before you draw, write the one sentence the user should leave with and one line per slide. Drop each slide that does not change what the user knows. Use at most 10 slides, and fewer is better. Neither note goes in the reply.

Each slide makes one point. Build the slides on one picture and change it from slide to slide, rather than cut to an unrelated one. A color or shape means the same thing on every slide. Show the work in pictures, such as how a system works, a PR stack with each PR's state, a before and after of a fix, or options side by side. Point at what changed with arrows, highlights, and motion. A slide is never a bullet list, a status list, a table, or boxes with nothing connecting them.

Keep text to the minimum. Put each label on the thing it names, in plain words a person would say, not tags like "Tests: pass". Never caption what a picture shows, and never give a fact twice, across slides or in the text under them.

Show what came of each thing the user asked for, and whether it is done, still running, failed, or not checked. Never show something done that still waits on the user. Show any bug found and fixed on the way. If a visible change has no screenshot, say so on its slide and name what changed on screen. Draw only from facts you have. Never invent a number, and never draw a screen you did not capture.

## Subtitles

Give each slide one short subtitle line, about ten words, that says its point the way you would tell the user, from only what that slide shows. Say what the picture means rather than read out its labels. Put the subtitle in a strip below the slide, the same height on every slide and empty except for the subtitle, so it never covers the slide. Set it small, but readable when the video plays at chat size.

## Render

Choose the rendering tools. Produce one silent landscape video file, such as an mp4, with motion between slides and burned-in subtitles. Every frame is the slide itself, edge to edge, above its subtitle strip. Make the frames from the slides, never by recording a screen, and burn each subtitle into its slide's frames so it stays on screen for the slide's whole hold. The hold is the time a slide is fully on screen, not counting the motion into or out of it. Hold each slide long enough to read the words it adds, at least one second for each three words. A slide adds its subtitle and each word that was not on the slide before it, including a label it changes. The first slide adds all its words. Words inside a screenshot do not count.

Before you embed the video, check the rendered file. Look at one frame from each slide at chat size, and check that its subtitle is readable and covers nothing. Measure each hold by matching the file's frames to the slide, not with a still-frame detector, which counts the faint ends of a fade as still. Count the words each slide adds against its measured hold. Fix any slide you cannot read in that time, render again, and check again.
